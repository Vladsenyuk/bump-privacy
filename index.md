# Bump: privacy policy

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Bump is an app for adding a friend by touching two phones together. This page says what Bump does
with information about you.

The short version: Bump has no account, no server, and no permission to use the internet. Its
developer never receives anything from your phone.

## What Bump keeps on your phone

- The nickname, motto and pictures you choose.
- The people you have bumped: the nickname, motto and pictures their phone sent, when you met, and
  how often.
- The messages you write and receive, and the events you are in.
- The keys that seal your messages. They are made on your phone.

## What goes to another phone

Bump talks to other phones directly, over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, and only to phones that are near you
with Bump open.

- **To a nearby phone with Bump open:** a random code, new every time you open Bump, and the sensor
  readings needed to recognise a bump: which way your phone points, the moment and strength of a
  knock, the air pressure, and how strongly it hears the other phone. None of this carries your name.
- **To a phone you bump:** your nickname, motto and pictures, how many friends and meetups you have,
  and which friends the two of you have in common. A phone learns nothing about your other friends
  except how many of them its owner has not met.
- **To a friend, or to the members of an event you are in:** the messages you write, sealed so that
  only their phones can read them.

What another person's phone has received stays on that phone. Bump cannot take it back.

## What Bump does not do

- It has no account and asks for no email address or phone number.
- It shows no ads and contains no analytics or tracking.
- It sends nothing to its developer or to any company. It cannot: it has no permission to use the
  internet.

Bump finds and talks to nearby phones through Nearby Connections, which is part of Google Play
services on your phone. Google's own privacy policy covers that component.

## What it asks permission for

- **Nearby devices, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi:** to find and talk to the phone in front of you.
- **Precise location:** Android requires it before an app may look for nearby phones. Bump also
  reads your phone's last known position to check its compass against the Earth's magnetic field.
  The position is used on your phone, and is not stored or sent anywhere.
- **Notifications:** only if you turn them on, to tell you when a friend nudges you or somebody near
  you opens Bump.

## Backups

If your phone's backup to your Google account is switched on, or you copy your phone to a new one,
Android takes one small file from Bump with it: an encryption key. Nothing else from Bump is backed
up or copied.

## Deleting your information

- "Clear All My Bumps" in Settings removes the people you have met from your phone.
- Uninstalling Bump removes everything it kept on your phone.
- There is nothing to delete anywhere else, because nothing was sent to a server.

## Children

Bump is not directed at children.
